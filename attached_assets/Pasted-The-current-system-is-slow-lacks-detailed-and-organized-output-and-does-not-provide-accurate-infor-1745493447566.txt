Block Definitions and Structures:

Block 1: Success Rate Analysis





Purpose: Estimate the likelihood of the startup's success in the selected country and category.



Structure:





Success Probability (0-100%): Provide a percentage with a brief justification.



Key Factors: List 3-5 factors influencing success (e.g., market demand, competition intensity).



Data Sources: Reference industry trends or similar startups (if available).



Example: "75% success probability due to high demand for [category] in [country]. Factors: growing middle class, low regulatory barriers."

Block 2: Competitors & Market Share





Purpose: Identify key competitors and their market dominance.



Structure:





Top Competitors: List 3-5 major players in the category and country.



Market Share: Estimate their market share (percentage or qualitative description if data is unavailable).



Competitive Advantage: Highlight gaps or opportunities for the new startup.



Example: "Competitors: [Company A (40%), Company B (25%)]. Opportunity: underserved rural markets."

Block 3: Target Audience Fit





Purpose: Assess how well the idea aligns with the target demographic.



Structure:





Primary Audience: Define age, income, preferences, and behaviors.



Fit Analysis: Explain how the idea meets audience needs (3-5 points).



Risks: Note potential mismatches or challenges.



Example: "Target: 18-35 urban professionals. Fit: affordable pricing, mobile-first solution. Risk: low brand loyalty."

Block 4: Market Size Analysis





Purpose: Quantify the market opportunity in the selected country.



Structure:





Total Addressable Market (TAM): Estimate in USD or local currency.



Serviceable Addressable Market (SAM): Narrow down to the startup’s reachable market.



Growth Trends: Highlight 3-5 year market growth projections.



Example: "TAM: $500M. SAM: $50M. Growth: 10% CAGR due to digital adoption."

Block 5: Business Model Strength





Purpose: Evaluate the viability of the proposed business model.



Structure:





Business Model Type: Identify (e.g., subscription, freemium).



Strengths: List 3-5 advantages (e.g., scalability, recurring revenue).



Weaknesses: Note 1-3 potential flaws or challenges.



Example: "Model: Subscription. Strengths: predictable revenue, high retention. Weakness: high customer acquisition cost."

Block 6: Funding Requirements





Purpose: Estimate capital needed to launch and sustain the startup.



Structure:





Initial Funding: Estimate for first 12-18 months (in USD or local currency).



Breakdown: Allocate to key areas (e.g., tech development, marketing).



Funding Sources: Suggest options (e.g., VC, bootstrapping).



Example: "Initial: $200K. Breakdown: 50% tech, 30% marketing. Sources: angel investors, grants."

Block 7: SWOT Analysis





Purpose: Provide a strategic overview of internal and external factors.



Structure:





Strengths: 3-5 internal advantages.



Weaknesses: 3-5 internal challenges.



Opportunities: 3-5 external possibilities.



Threats: 3-5 external risks.



Example: "Strengths: innovative tech, experienced team. Weaknesses: limited brand awareness. Opportunities: untapped markets. Threats: regulatory changes."

Block 8: Previous Failed Executions





Purpose: Highlight lessons from similar failed startups.



Structure:





Case Studies: Summarize 1-3 failed startups in the category/country.



Reasons for Failure: List key causes (e.g., poor market fit, underfunding).



Lessons Learned: Provide 2-3 actionable takeaways.



Example: "Case: [Startup X] failed due to high pricing. Lesson: prioritize affordability."
